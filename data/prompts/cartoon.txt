CRITICAL — what makes a great cartoon:
- Great single-panel cartoons find a SURPRISING connection between two unrelated ideas. Don't just illustrate a logistics frustration literally — find an unexpected angle, an absurd comparison, a twist.
- The best single-panel cartoons work through MISDIRECTION. The viewer thinks they're looking at one thing, then the caption (or a small detail) reveals it's something else entirely.
- Do NOT anthropomorphise containers, ships, or cargo. That's the lazy version of this joke. No containers sitting in chairs. No ships with feelings. The comedy should come from PEOPLE and SITUATIONS.
- Do NOT do "logistics thing but it's a [doctor/funeral/therapy session]" — that's one-note and predictable. Find fresher frames.
- Think about RELATIONSHIPS and POWER DYNAMICS: the forwarder vs the carrier, the ops team vs sales, the consultant vs reality, the customer vs the truth. The funniest cartoons capture a dynamic, not just a situation.
- Captions should have a TWIST — the reader should have to do a tiny bit of work to get the joke. If the caption just describes what's in the image, it's not clever enough.
- Dark is good but CLEVER is better. Wit over weight.
